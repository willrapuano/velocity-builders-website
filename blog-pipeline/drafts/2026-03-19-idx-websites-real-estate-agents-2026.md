# IDX Websites for Real Estate Agents: The 2026 Setup Guide

If you're a real estate agent without an IDX-enabled website in 2026, you're sending every organic lead to Zillow, Realtor.com, and Redfin — and then paying to get them back. An IDX (Internet Data Exchange) website lets you display live MLS listings directly on your own site, capture leads on your domain, and build local search authority around the neighborhoods where you actually work.

But not all IDX setups are created equal. The platform you choose, how you configure the feed, and where you invest in content will determine whether your website generates leads or just looks professional while Zillow eats your lunch.

This guide covers what IDX is, how to set it up in 2026, which platforms work best for different agent types, and the SEO structure that turns IDX pages into lead-generating machines.

---

## What Is IDX and Why Does It Matter?

IDX is a policy and data-sharing framework that allows MLS member brokers and agents to display each other's listings on their websites. When you add IDX to your site, you get a live, searchable property feed — updated every 15 minutes to several hours depending on your MLS — that visitors can browse without leaving your domain.

**Why this matters for lead generation:**

- **You keep the lead.** Someone searching "homes for sale in Vienna VA" finds your IDX page instead of Zillow's. They register on your site, not theirs.
- **You build SEO authority.** Every listing page, neighborhood search page, and market data page is indexable content that competes for local keywords.
- **You control the experience.** Your branding, your CTAs, your lead capture forms — not a portal's.
- **You reduce ad dependency.** Organic traffic from IDX pages compounds over time. Paid leads stop the moment you stop paying.

## IDX Platform Options in 2026

### Option 1: Dedicated Real Estate Platforms with Built-In IDX
These platforms are built specifically for real estate and include IDX as a core feature.

**Examples:** Sierra Interactive, Real Geeks, Luxury Presence, Jeeves (kvCORE), Lofty (formerly Chime)

**Pros:**
- IDX is native — no plugins or third-party integrations
- Built-in CRM, lead capture, and drip campaigns
- Designed for real estate workflows
- Fast setup (days, not weeks)

**Cons:**
- Limited design customization
- You're locked into their ecosystem
- SEO flexibility varies significantly by platform
- Monthly costs: $200–$1,500/mo depending on features

**Best for:** Agents who want an all-in-one solution and don't need a custom-designed site.

### Option 2: Custom Website + IDX API Feed
Build a custom website (Next.js, WordPress, etc.) and connect to your MLS through an IDX data provider like Repliers, Spark, or Trestle.

**Pros:**
- Full design and UX control
- Superior SEO flexibility — you control every URL, meta tag, and schema markup
- Can build custom neighborhood pages, market data dashboards, and conversion funnels
- Own your tech stack — no platform lock-in

**Cons:**
- Higher upfront development cost ($5K–$25K)
- Requires technical maintenance
- MLS compliance responsibility falls on you
- IDX feed setup takes 2–4 weeks for data access approval

**Best for:** Teams serious about SEO and long-term lead generation who want a unique digital presence.

### Option 3: IDX Plugin on Existing Website
Add IDX to a WordPress, Squarespace, or Wix site using a plugin or iframe widget like IDX Broker, Showcase IDX, or iHomefinder.

**Pros:**
- Keep your existing website
- Lower cost ($40–$100/mo for the IDX plugin)
- Quick to add

**Cons:**
- iFrame-based IDX is terrible for SEO (Google doesn't index iframed content well)
- Limited customization of the search experience
- Performance issues — slow load times, clunky mobile experience
- "Bolted on" feel that hurts credibility

**Best for:** Agents who need basic listing search and aren't prioritizing organic traffic.

**The Squarespace IDX problem:** This comes up a lot. Squarespace doesn't natively support IDX. You can add it via iframe embed or a subdomain, but neither option gives you real SEO value. If SEO matters to your business, Squarespace + IDX is not the answer.

## How to Set Up an IDX Website: Step by Step

### Step 1: Confirm MLS IDX Access
Contact your MLS to confirm you have IDX access rights. Most MLSs require:
- Active broker/agent membership
- Signed IDX data agreement
- Compliance with MLS display rules (disclaimers, data attribution, update frequency)

For NoVA agents on BrightMLS, IDX access is included with membership but requires a separate data license agreement.

### Step 2: Choose Your IDX Data Provider
Your IDX data provider handles the MLS feed — pulling listings, formatting data, and delivering it to your website.

**Key providers in 2026:**
| Provider | Feed Type | SEO Value | Typical Cost |
|----------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| Repliers | RETS/API | High (individual URLs) | $99–$249/mo |
| Spark (by FBS) | RETS/API | High | $50–$150/mo |
| IDX Broker | iFrame + organic | Medium | $40–$100/mo |
| Trestle (by CoreLogic) | RESO Web API | High | Varies |

**What to ask your provider:**
- How often does the feed update? (Every 15 min is standard; hourly is too slow)
- Do listing pages get unique, indexable URLs? (Critical for SEO)
- Can you customize the property detail page layout?
- What MLS compliance features are built in? (Disclaimers, data attribution)

### Step 3: Build Your Site Structure for SEO
This is where most agents leave money on the table. A good IDX site isn't just a property search — it's an SEO content machine.

**URL structure that ranks:**
```
/homes-for-sale/fairfax-county/
/homes-for-sale/fairfax-county/vienna/
/homes-for-sale/fairfax-county/vienna/windover-heights/
/homes-for-sale/loudoun-county/ashburn/brambleton/
```

**Each location page should include:**
- H1: "Homes for Sale in [Neighborhood], [City] VA"
- Live IDX listing feed filtered to that area
- 300–500 words of neighborhood content (schools, commute, lifestyle)
- Market snapshot (median price, days on market, inventory)
- Internal links to related neighborhoods and city pages
- Lead capture form specific to that area

**This creates a content hierarchy:**
County hub → City pages → Neighborhood pages → Individual listings

Google rewards this structure because it demonstrates topical authority and serves genuine user intent.

### Step 4: Configure Lead Capture
Your IDX site needs lead capture at multiple touchpoints:

- **Registration walls** — Require registration after 3–5 property views (not immediately — let them browse first)
- **Saved search alerts** — Offer automated email alerts for new listings matching their criteria
- **Property inquiry forms** — On every listing detail page
- **CTA buttons** — "Schedule a showing," "Get pre-approved," "Ask about this home"
- **Landing pages** — Neighborhood-specific pages with gated market reports

**Route every lead to your CRM.** If your IDX captures leads but doesn't push them into your CRM with proper tagging and automated [follow-up](/blog/real-estate-follow-up-email-templates), you're building a list you'll never work.

### Step 5: Handle MLS Compliance
Every MLS has display rules. Violating them can get your IDX access revoked. Common requirements:

- **Listing attribution** — Display the listing broker name and MLS number
- **Data freshness disclaimer** — "Last updated: [timestamp]"
- **MLS logo/copyright notice** — Usually in the footer
- **No caching beyond MLS rules** — Most MLSs require data updates at least every 4 hours; BrightMLS requires 15-minute refresh maximum
- **Opt-out compliance** — Some brokers opt out of IDX; their listings must not appear on your site

Your IDX provider should handle most of this automatically, but verify.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Using iFrame IDX and expecting SEO results.** Google can't index content inside iframes reliably. If SEO matters, use an API-based IDX feed with indexable URLs.

2. **Building an IDX site with no content.** Listing data alone doesn't rank. You need neighborhood descriptions, market analysis, and local expertise content.

3. **Ignoring mobile performance.** 60%+ of real estate search traffic is mobile. If your IDX pages load slowly or the search filters are clunky on a phone, you're losing leads.

4. **Registration wall too early.** Forcing registration on the first property view kills bounce rate. Let users browse 3–5 listings first, then gate.

5. **No CRM connection.** Capturing leads without automated follow-up is like building a bucket with a hole in it.
