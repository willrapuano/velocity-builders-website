# The Real Estate Follow-Up Email Templates That Actually Book Consults

Here's the uncomfortable truth about real estate follow-up: most agents have one. They send a "Hey, just checking in!" email, maybe twice, and then the lead goes cold. The agent blames the lead quality. The lead found another agent who actually followed up.

The problem isn't the lead. It's the follow-up.

This post gives you 12 real estate follow-up email templates you can copy, paste, and load into your CRM today. Each one is designed for a specific stage of the pipeline and a specific trigger — so you're not just "touching base." You're advancing a conversation toward a consult.

---

## Why Most Follow-Up Emails Fail

Before we get to the templates, let's diagnose the disease:

**Problem 1: No urgency.** "Just checking in" is the email equivalent of standing in a doorway. It doesn't move anything forward.

**Problem 2: No value.** If your email doesn't teach something, answer something, or offer something the lead cares about, it's noise.

**Problem 3: No structure.** Random follow-ups at random intervals signal disorganization. A structured sequence signals professionalism.

**Problem 4: No trigger.** The best follow-ups are sent because something happened — a new listing, a price drop, a market shift. Not because a calendar reminder told you to "reach out."

The templates below solve all four.

## Stage 1: New Lead — First 24 Hours

### Template 1: The Speed-to-Lead Response (Immediate)

**Subject:** Quick question about [Area/Property Type]

Hi [First Name],

Thanks for reaching out. Quick question so I can point you in the right direction:

Are you actively looking to [buy/sell] in the next 90 days, or are you earlier in the research phase?

Either way is totally fine — it just helps me send you stuff that's actually useful instead of generic updates.

Talk soon,
[Agent Name]

**Why it works:** It's short, asks one question, and immediately segments the lead by timeline. Most agents send a novel. This sends a question.

---

### Template 2: The Value-First Follow-Up (Hour 4–6)

**Subject:** [Area] market data you might want

Hi [First Name],

Since you're looking at [Area], here's what you should know right now:

- Median days on market: [X] days
- Average list-to-sale ratio: [X]%
- Active inventory: [X] homes in your price range

This changes week to week. Want me to set up automatic alerts when something hits the market that matches what you're looking for?

[Agent Name]

**Why it works:** It delivers specific, local value before asking for anything. The lead gets useful data and a low-commitment offer (market alerts).

---

### Template 3: The Consult Pivot (Day 2)

**Subject:** Thought of you — quick idea

Hi [First Name],

I pulled a few properties that match what you mentioned. Rather than guessing what matters most to you (commute? schools? yard space?), it'd be faster to jump on a 15-minute call so I can narrow it down.

Here's my calendar: [Link]
Or just reply with a time that works and I'll call you.

[Agent Name]

**Why it works:** It assumes forward motion and offers a specific, low-commitment next step. "15-minute call" is less intimidating than "consultation."

## Stage 2: Post-Showing — 24–72 Hours After

### Template 4: The Specific Feedback Ask

**Subject:** Quick thought on [Property Address]

Hi [First Name],

Now that you've had a day to think about [Address] — what's your gut? I'm curious whether the [specific feature they reacted to: kitchen layout / backyard size / commute distance] landed the way you hoped.

No pressure either way. If it's not the one, I've got two more I want to show you that solve the [issue they mentioned].

[Agent Name]

**Why it works:** It references something specific from the showing, which signals you were paying attention. It also pre-loads the next showing.

---

### Template 5: The Competitive Urgency Nudge

**Subject:** Update on [Property Address]

Hi [First Name],

Heads up — [Address] has had [X] showings in the last [X] days. I'm not trying to pressure you, but if it's on your short list, we should talk about timing and strategy before it moves.

Want to chat for 5 minutes tomorrow?

[Agent Name]

**Why it works:** Real urgency based on real data. It's not manufactured pressure — it's market context the buyer needs to make a decision.

## Stage 3: Stale Pipeline — 14–60 Days Cold

### Template 6: The Pattern Interrupt

**Subject:** Am I annoying you yet?

Hi [First Name],

I've sent a few messages and haven't heard back — which usually means one of three things:

1. You found another agent (totally fine, just let me know and I'll stop reaching out)
2. Your timeline shifted and you're not ready yet
3. My emails are going to spam and you're wondering why I ghosted you

Any of the above? A one-word reply works. I just don't want to waste your time if the situation's changed.

[Agent Name]

**Why it works:** It's honest, self-aware, and gives the lead an easy out. Counterintuitively, this template gets more replies than any "just checking in" email because it breaks the pattern.

---

### Template 7: The Re-Engagement with New Value

**Subject:** Market just shifted in [Area] — worth knowing

Hi [First Name],

Quick update: [specific market change — new inventory spike, price correction, rate movement, new construction announcement] in [Area].

If you're still thinking about [buying/selling], this could change the math on timing. Want me to run updated numbers for you?

[Agent Name]

**Why it works:** It re-engages with a trigger event, not a calendar reminder. The lead sees value in opening the email.

---

## Stage 4: Post-Close — Retention & Referral

### Template 8: The 30-Day Check-In

**Subject:** Everything settling in?

Hi [First Name],

You've been in the house for about a month now. How's it going? Any surprises — good or bad?

Two quick things:
1. If anything's not right, I have contacts for every trade: plumber, electrician, HVAC, handyman. Just ask.
2. If anyone you know mentions buying or selling, I'd be grateful for the introduction. No pressure — just putting it out there.

Enjoy the new place.

[Agent Name]

**Why it works:** Genuine check-in + soft referral ask. The home services offer provides immediate value.

---

### Template 9: The Anniversary Trigger

**Subject:** Happy house-iversary 🏠

Hi [First Name],

One year ago today, you closed on [Address]. Time flies.

Quick thought: your home has likely gained [estimated value change] based on recent comps in [Neighborhood]. Want me to pull a quick equity snapshot for you? Takes 5 minutes and it's free.

[Agent Name]

**Why it works:** Automated, personal, and provides real value. The equity snapshot opens a conversation about their next move.

## Stage 5: Agent-to-Lender / Partner Follow-Up

### Template 10: The Co-Marketing Pitch

**Subject:** Idea for your [Area] buyers

Hi [Loan Officer Name],

I've been noticing a lot of [first-time buyer / move-up / VA] activity in [Area]. I've got a content system that generates local leads — and I'm looking for a lending partner to co-brand with on [buyer guides / rate update emails / open house promos].

Here's what I'm thinking:
- I provide the local content and lead flow
- You provide the rate context and pre-approval speed
- We split the marketing costs and share the leads

Interested? 15 minutes on the phone and I'll walk you through the model.

[Agent Name]

**Why it works:** Specific, value-driven, and structured. It's not "let's grab coffee" — it's "here's a business model."

## How to Deploy These Templates

### Step 1: Map to Your Pipeline Stages
Every template corresponds to a pipeline stage. Map them to your CRM:
- **New Lead** → Templates 1–3 (automated sequence, days 1–2)
- **Post-Showing** → Templates 4–5 (manual or triggered by showing confirmation)
- **Stale Pipeline** → Templates 6–7 (automated at 14-day and 30-day marks)
- **Post-Close** → Templates 8–9 (automated at 30 days and 12 months)
- **Partner** → Template 10 (manual, relationship-driven)

### Step 2: Customize the Variables
Every template has brackets — [Area], [Property Address], [First Name]. Don't skip these. Generic templates get deleted. Personalized templates get replies.

### Step 3: Set the Triggers
The best [follow-up systems](/services) aren't calendar-based — they're event-based:
- New lead enters CRM → Template 1 fires immediately
- Showing confirmed → Template 4 queues for next day
- No response for 14 days → Template 6 auto-sends
- Closing recorded → Template 8 schedules for 30 days out

### Step 4: Measure What Matters
Track these metrics monthly:
- **Open rate by template** (target: 35%+)
- **Reply rate by template** (target: 8%+)
- **Consults booked from follow-up** (the number that actually matters)

## The Math on Follow-Up

A [study from the National Association of Realtors](https://www.nar.realtor/) shows that 80% of sales require 5+ follow-up contacts. Most agents stop after 2.

If you're working 50 leads per month and your current follow-up books consults with 5% of them, you're booking 2.5 consults. Improving follow-up to book 12% of leads gives you 6 consults — from the same lead flow.

That's not a marketing problem. That's a systems problem. And systems are fixable.
